4:Teb6,<h1>710 Tasks and the Bottleneck That Wasn't</h1>
<p>My agent swarm shares a task board. Agents pick up work, do it, mark it done. Simple. Except one morning the board showed <strong>710 open items</strong>, and I had a story ready for why: the bots generate faster than I can review, so everything piles up behind me. The human is the bottleneck. Obviously.</p>
<p>I was about to go build a fancier review queue to fix my supposed slowness when I did the thing I should have done first. I counted.</p>
<h2 id="the-five-minute-query-that-killed-my-assumption">The five-minute query that killed my assumption</h2>
<p>"Open" is a single word hiding several different situations. So I cohorted the 710 by the actual reason each item was still sitting there:</p>
<p>| Cohort | Roughly | Whose problem | |---|---|---| | Worker failed (crashed, or exited without reporting done) | ~55% | Reliability | | Stale cruft from an old migration, never triaged | ~25% | Nobody's — delete it | | Genuinely waiting on me to decide something | <strong>69</strong> | Mine | | Blocked on another task | the rest | Sequencing |</p>
<p>Sixty-nine. My real decision queue, the set of things that actually could not move without a human, was <strong>69 items out of 710</strong>. The other ninety-plus percent had nothing to do with me. The biggest single slice was workers dying mid-task, and the next was landfill from a migration months earlier that no one had ever swept. Not a queue. Sediment.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-failures-actually-were">What the failures actually were</h2>
<p>Two signatures accounted for most of the dead workers:</p>
<ul><li><strong>"Exited cleanly without reporting done."</strong> The agent finished its work and then just...</li></ul>
<p>  stopped, without calling the one function that marks the task complete. The system correctly   counted that as a failure. The work may even have happened, but nothing recorded it. One   protocol bug, dozens of stuck tasks.</p>
<ul><li><strong>"Iteration budget exhausted."</strong> Tasks too big for one worker to finish in its turn budget.</li></ul>
<p>  The fix here is not bigger budgets, which just burns more tokens hitting the same wall. It's   decomposition: break the task down so each piece fits.</p>
<p>Neither of those is a review problem. Both were invisible because "open" looked like one undifferentiated pile, and the pile *felt* like it was my fault.</p>
<h2 id="the-lesson-worth-stealing">The lesson worth stealing</h2>
<p>The most expensive assumption in an autonomous system is the one you never check because it flatters your mental model. "The human is the bottleneck" felt true, so I almost spent a day building the wrong fix for it.</p>
<p>Three habits that came out of it:</p>
<ol><li><strong>Cohort before you conclude.</strong> A single status like "open" or "blocked" is a category, not</li></ol>
<p>   a cause. The five-minute query that splits it by *why* is worth more than the dashboard that    shows you the total.</p>
<ol><li><strong>The real queue is smaller than the scary number.</strong> Mine was 69, not 710. Find the number</li></ol>
<p>   that's genuinely yours before you redesign your life around the one that isn't.</p>
<ol><li><strong>Silent worker death is the default failure mode.</strong> Agents don't usually crash loudly. They</li></ol>
<p>   finish-and-forget, or hit a wall and sit. If you're not measuring completion as a distinct    thing from "didn't error," you're not measuring it at all.</p>
<p>I still owe my board a real reliability fix: the report-on-completion bug, the decomposition routing, a broom for the migration sediment. But at least now I'm building the fix for the problem I *have*, not the one that happened to make me the hero of the story.</p>b:X
