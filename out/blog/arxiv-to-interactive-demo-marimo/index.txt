20:T199c,<h1>From arXiv Paper to Interactive Demo in 20 Minutes</h1>
<p>I read a lot of papers. Most of them sit in a tab until I forget why I opened them. The ones that stick are the ones I can *play with*; tweak a parameter, see the curve shift, develop an intuition for why the method works.</p>
<p>The problem: turning a paper into a playground usually takes hours. Jupyter notebooks are stateful JSON blobs that break when you look at them wrong. Streamlit apps are one-off scripts that don't compose. And most "reproducibility" efforts are Docker containers that take 30 minutes to build and require a GPU you don't have.</p>
<p>Last week we tried something different. We took the classic multi-armed bandit problem; the explore/exploit dilemma that underlies every routing decision our agents make; and built an interactive demo in about 20 minutes. The tool is <strong>marimo</strong>, and it's now part of our research pipeline.</p>
<p>---</p>
<h2 id="what-marimo-actually-is">What marimo Actually Is</h2>
<p>marimo is a reactive Python notebook environment. Unlike Jupyter, marimo notebooks are plain .py files with a reactive DAG execution model. Change a slider, and every cell that depends on it re-runs automatically. No manual re-execution, no hidden state, no "run all cells" anxiety.</p>
<p>The killer features for us:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Notebooks are .py files</strong>; they diff cleanly in git, they run as CLI scripts, they import like normal modules</li><li><strong>Reactive execution</strong>; cells auto-update when their dependencies change, like a spreadsheet but for code</li><li><strong>Script mode</strong>; the same notebook runs headless (for cron jobs or batch experiments) or interactively (in the browser)</li><li><strong>WASM deployment</strong>; notebooks can run entirely in the client's browser via Pyodide, zero server cost</li></ul>
<p>---</p>
<h2 id="the-demo-multi-armed-bandits">The Demo: Multi-Armed Bandits</h2>
<p>The paper concept is simple: you have K slot machines ("arms") with unknown payout distributions. Each pull gives you a sample from one arm. Your goal is to maximize total reward over N pulls.</p>
<p>The tension is between <strong>exploration</strong> (trying arms to learn their payouts) and <strong>exploitation</strong> (pulling the arm you currently believe is best). This is the same trade-off every AI agent faces when choosing which skill to invoke, which data source to query, or which market to trade.</p>
<p>We built a notebook comparing three strategies:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Greedy</strong>: Always pull the arm with the highest sample mean. Zero exploration. Gets stuck in local optima.</li><li><strong>$\epsilon$-Greedy</strong>: Explore randomly with probability $\epsilon$, exploit otherwise. Works but wastes pulls on obviously bad arms.</li><li><strong>Thompson Sampling</strong>: Maintain a Bayesian posterior over each arm's mean, sample from the posterior, and pull the winner. Naturally balances exploration and exploitation.</li></ul>
<p>The notebook has sliders for arm count, exploration rate, number of pulls, and Monte Carlo runs. Hit "Run Experiment" and it produces two plots: cumulative regret over time, and a bar chart of average regret in the final window.</p>
<p>The results are immediate and visceral. Thompson Sampling consistently achieves 30-60% lower cumulative regret than Greedy. You can see why: Greedy agents lock onto the first decent arm they find and never question it. Thompson Sampling maintains uncertainty and lets high-variance arms compete until the evidence is clear.</p>
<p>---</p>
<h2 id="what-this-means-for-edgeless">What This Means for Edgeless</h2>
<p>We now have a pipeline:</p>
<ol><li><strong>Intake</strong>: Beau or Hive spots an interesting paper during RSS/YouTube triage</li><li><strong>Fetch</strong>: AlphaXiv gives us structured markdown of the paper (no PDF parsing)</li><li><strong>Demo</strong>: marimo auto skill picks the core concept and builds an interactive notebook</li><li><strong>Publish</strong>: The notebook goes into our vault, indexed by ChromaDB, linked to related KB articles</li><li><strong>Deploy</strong>: WASM-compatible demos can be embedded in blog posts or shared as standalone URLs</li></ol>
<p>The implications:</p>
<p><strong>For research</strong>: We don't just read papers; we *instrument* them. Every paper in our KB can have a live demo attached. This makes the knowledge actionable, also not only archived.</p>
<p><strong>For clients</strong>: We can build interactive dashboards and training tools in marimo instead of Streamlit or custom web apps. The same notebook that trains a model can become the client's monitoring dashboard with zero extra work.</p>
<p><strong>For the swarm</strong>: Our cron jobs can be marimo notebooks. When they break, we open them in the browser, adjust a slider, and debug interactively instead of reading log files.</p>
<p>---</p>
<h2 id="what-s-next">What's Next</h2>
<p>We're building demos for three more paper categories:</p>
<ul><li><strong>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)</strong>; skip the reward model, optimize directly from preferences. The math is clean and the comparison to RLHF is visually striking.</li><li><strong>Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)</strong>; DeepSeek's approach to reasoning without a critic. The core insight (group rewards replace individual value estimates) is perfect for a toy grid-world demo.</li><li><strong>Self-Evolving Agent Memory</strong>; population-based memory broadcast without weight updates. Relevant to our swarm's memory architecture.</li></ul>
<p>Each demo is a .py file that runs standalone, lives in our vault, and links back to the paper metadata via ChromaDB.</p>
<p>---</p>
<h2 id="try-it">Try It</h2>
<p>The bandit notebook is a single file: <a href="https://github.com/thedavidmurray/marimo-demos/blob/main/bandit-strategies.py">bandit-strategies.py</a></p>
<p>Run it locally:</p>
<pre><code>uv run --with marimo --with numpy --with matplotlib marimo run bandit-strategies.py</code></pre>
<p>Or open it in the browser as a WASM app (no install, no server):</p>
<p><a href="https://molab.marimo.io/github/thedavidmurray/marimo-demos/blob/main/bandit-strategies.py">Open in molab</a></p>
<p>The source repo has the full set of skills we use to build these demos automatically from arXiv IDs.</p>
